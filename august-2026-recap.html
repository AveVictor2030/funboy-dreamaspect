FUNBOY Social Recap

Instagram and TikTok, August 1-31, 2026. Report prepared by dreamaspect.co.

Overview

August content centered on the late-summer transition into the Halloween Collection launch, supported by two content shoots — July 31 (studio) and Thursday, August 13 (lifestyle).

The teaser week and launch (Aug 12–18) performed really well: Week 3 (Aug 15–21), which captured the tail of the teaser run plus the Aug 18 launch, averaged 367 likes and 50.6 comments per post — more than double August's overall average (166 likes, 23.2 comments) and 3-4.5x Weeks 1 and 2 (81 and 115 likes).

Another win this month was TikTok activation led by Natalie and Ellie — their post outperformed everything else posted in August.

Below, we review what worked well and what didn't perform as expected. A more in-depth analysis of what needs improvement will be published in September.

What Worked

  • The teaser countdown. Five straight days of Halloween teasers (Aug 12-18) led into the month's best week, Week 3 (Aug 15-21).
  • Behind-the-scenes posts. Aug 14, 17, and 20 all landed above the month's average likes.
  • Comment-bait captions. Comments per post ran 53% above the 2-month trend this month.
  • Non-follower discovery reach. Organic posts already draw more views from non-followers than followers on average (53% non-follower share) — three pulled well above that baseline: Aug 23 (68.4% non-follower), Aug 22 (67.6%), and Aug 7 (64.4%), each flagged "High non-follower reach" in the grid above.
  • The TikTok gifting post. "DM us for a chance to receive a spooky setup" was TikTok's best post of the month by a wide margin, though it's a gifting mechanic, not organic, so it's excluded from TikTok's averages.
  • Customer content. Several tagged posts landed 1,000-7,000+ likes, and one TikTok video from a customer hit 60,100 likes, more than anything we posted ourselves.

What Didn't

  • Early August was the softest stretch. Aug 1 and Aug 2 had the lowest likes of Week 1 (68 and 71).
    Learnings: our hypothesis is that repurposed content without strong context tends to underperform compared to the average.
  • The Aug 6 meme post. Lowest likes of the entire month (26). The humor format didn't land this time — that's the one thing we can say for sure.
    Learnings: we're not concluding that meme posts don't work in general — this particular video likely lacked a strong punchline or ending.
  • Engagement cooled after the Aug 18 launch. Weeks 4 and 5 (Aug 22-31) averaged 124 and 118 likes vs Week 3's 367 (excl. the boosted post), even with more Halloween content posted on Aug 19, 20, and 22.
    Learnings: next time, distribute content more evenly after launch so the following weeks hold closer to launch-week performance.

Learnings

  1. Repeat the multi-day teaser format for the next launch. It's the clearest lever in this month's numbers.
  2. Plan a follow-up push for the week after launch. Engagement was still above baseline post-launch, but well below the peak.
  3. Keep leaning into BTS content. It outperformed styled product shots this month.
  4. Repost strong customer content. It's outperforming our own posts by a wide margin.
  5. Dig into the reach split. Four posts (Aug 6, 7, 19, 30) skewed the opposite way, drawing 80%+ of their views from existing followers instead of new ones. Worth a closer look at what separates the non-follower-reach standouts from these, to replicate discovery wins going forward.
